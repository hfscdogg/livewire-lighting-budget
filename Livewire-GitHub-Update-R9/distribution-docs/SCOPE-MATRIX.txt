LIVEWIRE LIGHTING BUDGET SCOPE MATRIX
R9 scope coverage | November 17, 2026

The estimator carries lighting choices through fixtures, controls, wiring,
shared equipment and installation tasks. These are budgeting allowances, not
a construction takeoff, electrical design or manufacturer-approved bill of
materials. The repository catalog includes entered company prices. Unpriced configurations remain unresolved. Labor task times are populated with
preliminary estimating judgments at $112.50 per person-hour. Catalog allowances provide a planning price; final assembly and site compatibility
require review before ordering.

WHAT EACH PATH MODELS

1. Conventional warm-dim recessed lighting
   Modeled: separate new-construction, retrofit and remodel assemblies; fixed
   and adjustable requests; installation materials; installation-method labor;
   shared or separate controls; branch wiring or centralized load wiring;
   circuit assessment. Retrofit includes removal of the existing lamp/trim.
   Still verify: exact module, housing/mount, trim/optic and driver inclusions,
   ceiling ratings, aiming, dimming compatibility and load limits. DMF Artafex assemblies are mapped in the company catalog using the supplied module, housing and trim prices. Verify the assembly against the selected site condition.

2. Rania and Ketra recessed lighting
   Modeled: HomeWorks-only planning path, complete fixture assemblies, undimmed
   power, existing-feed conversion where relevant, command keypads, physical
   wireless endpoints, shared headend and commissioning. No phase dimmer is
   added to these intelligent fixtures.
   Still verify: exact generation and installation assembly. D2R remodel
   requires the documented non-IC/non-fire-rated conditions and clearances;
   a generic retrofit selection does not prove an available compatible kit. [2]

3. Lumaris recessed lighting
   Modeled: fixed downlight requests with RA3- or HomeWorks-specific mapping,
   undimmed power, command controls, network/headend and activation. Adjustable
   requests remain blocked.
   Still verify: retrofit-can compatibility or the complete canless assembly,
   ceiling/insulation conditions, system-specific model and RF placement. [3]

4. Undercabinet lighting
   Modeled: installed feet, independent zones, Lumaris warm-dim/tunable-white/RGB+tunable-white
   and Rania natural-white selections; tape/accessory allowance;
   driver/controller with compatible supply; low-voltage leads and their
   installation; mains feed; local/central control or intelligent command path.
   Still verify: actual segment lengths, outputs, voltage drop, controller
   generation, supply sharing, connectors/channels and purchasing waste.
   Equal-length zones are a budgeting assumption, not a validated layout; an
   internal driver-count override can raise the minimum after actual run review.
   An unknown driver limit remains unresolved. Unmapped Ketra tape is blocked. [4]

5. Owner-supplied decorative fixtures
   Modeled: mounting/hanging at existing or new locations, existing-fixture
   removal allowance, new box/support where selected, separate cable routing,
   dimmer/switch and explicit sharing with compatible conventional groups.
   Still verify: fixture weight, assembly complexity, support, lamps/driver,
   dimming compatibility, working height and owner delivery. Fixture purchase
   is excluded. Hanging, new-box work and cable work are separate tasks.

6. Owner-supplied ceiling fans and controls
   Modeled: retain an existing fan, replace a fan or install at a new location.
   Selected installation includes assembly, hanging, connection and balancing;
   replacement includes removal. New locations add support, wiring and applicable
   access work. Unchecked support includes a replacement fan-rated support
   allowance. Controls can be on/off or compatible wall speed control.
   Still verify: motor/receiver compatibility, load, neutral, mounting, ceiling
   conditions and owner-supplied equipment. Specialty handling needs a separate
   allowance. Fan equipment purchase remains excluded. [5]

7. Local controls, multi-location controls and hybrid keypads
   Modeled: one primary control per unique conventional load group; companion
   hardware and labor for additional control locations; plate allowances and
   new control-box fittings where selected. A hybrid offsets one local dimmer
   and its installation, while retaining circuit wiring and companions.
   Still verify: the assigned load, traveler/neutral wiring, gang/plate layout
   and compatible companion. A standard keypad does not replace a dimmer. [5]

8. Line-voltage and wired QS keypads
   Modeled: existing/new line-voltage keypad work or QS mounting/backbox,
   cable, installation, link termination, PDU demand and power infrastructure.
   Wired HomeWorks seeTouch follows the QS path. Existing QS cable can be
   selected for reuse, subject to review. New keypad locations add openings.
   Still verify: complete keypad/base/column assembly, conductors, model-specific
   PDU demand and included finish/engraving. Keypad quantity is a planning
   policy, not a manufacturer-required minimum for every room. [6]

9. Centralized HomeWorks load control
   Modeled: dimming/switching outputs grouped by named panel location; separate
   module types; panels, line-voltage harnesses, module feed circuits, load
   home runs and retrofit rewiring. Local dimmers are not charged for those
   same loads. Processor-to-panel QS routes and separate control enclosures
   are included unless explicit co-location covers the first panel connection.
   Still verify: actual current, load type, derating, feed grouping, spare
   channels, physical placement and wiring. Four outputs per module and four
   DPM positions per selected PD4 panel are count minima, not load approval.
   Local load controls remain the default. Centralized loads follow the
   keypad-only wall preference; a wired QS keypad alone does not require
   a lighting panel. [7]

10. RA3 and HomeWorks shared equipment
    Modeled: processor/gateway capacity minima, coverage overrides, required
    power, Ethernet routes, shared network accessories, installation and
    commissioning. RA3 includes entered Type A repeaters and their labor.
    HomeWorks supports wired QSX plus gateways or dual-radio processors; a
    wired processor is added when a dual-radio project also needs QS devices.
    QS device addresses, switch legs, processor links and aggregate power are
    tracked separately. System capacity issues block the range.
    Still verify: RF/floor coverage, port allocation, actual QS power groups,
    cable gauge/distance, enclosures and networking. Endpoint counts do not
    establish coverage or a valid power topology. Custom HomeWorks Type A
    additions require a separate validated design. [8][9]

11. Electrical infrastructure and access
    Modeled: ceiling and wall route facts, separate shared-equipment paths,
    circuit assessment, branch and low-voltage pulls, route preparation,
    fishing setup, access removal, undimmed-feed conversion and endpoint work.
    Fixture assembly and mounting follow open versus finished surfaces and
    housing reuse. Project work type does not establish cutting permission.
    Accessible attic/cavity paths, documented fishing, selective openings and
    continuous removal have distinct allowances. Unknown or blocked new-cable
    routes prevent release. No-access/no-cut conditions need a checked route,
    permission change or separately priced alternate design.
    Working height, occupied rooms, restricted movement and specialty conditions
    are captured separately. Above-12-foot/stairwell work, masonry, rated
    assemblies, structural changes and suspected hazardous material require a
    separate reviewed method and pricing. No repair or painting is included.
    Still verify: actual lengths, circuit capacity, approved opening locations,
    route overlaps, obstructions and responsibilities of other trades.

12. Project labor and travel
    Modeled: planning, one startup hour, per-device programming/activation, owner handoff,
    and preliminary installation task standards with task-specific condition
    factors. Travel uses one two-person crew at eight hours/day, with on-site
    person-hours / 16 rounded up and a two-visit minimum for open-framing rough/trim or shades.
    Completed tasks round up to 0.25 hour after quantities and conditions.
    Additional crew-day setup is included. Planning defaults off site;
    programming and activation default on site; the estimator can classify tasks. See LABOR-ASSUMPTIONS.txt.
    Still verify: rough/trim return visits, fragmented scheduling, owner-supply
    delays and off-site/on-site allocation. Beyond 163 miles requires review.

PROVISIONAL MEDIUM RUNS

Current software defaults translate "medium run" into 50 ft per local lighting
branch; 75 ft per load home run, QS keypad run, panel QS route, network node,
new circuit feeder or module feed; and 15 ft per ordinary driver lead.
For standard Lumaris packages, the controller-to-tape allowance is up to 10 ft
with the correct 3- or 6-conductor cable. Confirm connector and route limits.
These are explicit provisional allowances, not measured lengths or verified
company standards. An estimator must revise them for the actual project.
Long routes require a documented estimator review. A control zone, power
circuit, driver, keypad and physical cable route are different quantities.

WHAT REMAINS OUTSIDE AUTOMATIC DESIGN

- The program does not calculate an engineered load schedule, code compliance,
  actual RF layout, individual power-group topology or a fully measured BOM.
- Generic material packages still need inclusion checks. Complete fixture and
  controller/supply packages must not duplicate parts charged elsewhere.
  Bundled hardware must still have installation labor covered by an approved
  task standard. A zero labor standard needs a deliberate inclusion/exclusion.
- Existing headend/panel spare-capacity credits, legacy RPM/H48 systems,
  0-10 V/DALI paths, special integrations, service upgrades, permits, lifts,
  disposal, hazardous materials, structural/millwork work and visits beyond
  the current allowance need separate scope decisions; they are not silently included.
- Drywall/plaster repair and painting, decorative fixture purchase and fan
  equipment supply remain explicit exclusions. Selected fan installation is included. Required finish work for a
  selected product must still be assigned to the responsible trade.

WHEN A BUDGET RANGE CAN APPEAR

A standalone planning range requires complete priced scope, applicable task
standards and a feasible installation method. General design reminders do not
hold the planning range. Unchecked D2/D2R ceiling suitability is an estimator
follow-up before ordering; recorded incompatibility remains unresolved.
Unsupported equipment, missing quantities/prices, capacity issues and blocked
no-cut routes prevent a complete range. Shared customer release additionally
requires the recorded design/price approval. A review checkbox does not resolve
an impossible route or substitute for electrical design.

SHADES
Read SHADING-ASSUMPTIONS.txt for the complete shade path matrix, historical
selling ranges, power/control separation, package labor boundaries, site and
quote gates, and manufacturer references. Shading shares compatible system
headends, planning, setup and crew travel with lighting. No lighting DPM is
added for a shade drive. Standalone RF remote operation needs no lighting headend.

SHARED PROJECT QUALIFICATION

A salesperson can record an undecided control system or an addition to an
existing system. A range stays pending until the design and available capacity
are resolved. In shared projects, room controls default to distributed. Select
centralized control explicitly when a central lighting panel is in the design.
Wired HomeWorks keypads can also be used with distributed loads.

Lumaris fixed downlights and tape controllers have platform-specific part
numbers. The catalog keeps separate RadioRA 3 and HomeWorks price rows. Use
16.4 ft per standard tunable-white controller and up to 32.8 ft per RGB
controller on the stated power package, in parallel runs of at most 16.4 ft.
The estimate allows one compatible power package per controller. Sharing a
power interface or using a different controller requires checked design.
Lengths per control group can be entered when the groups are uneven. A new
15 A Lumaris circuit is budgeted for every started 40 downlights, subject to
verified circuit allocation and other loads. Sources: Lutron 3691335 and
3691301, https://assets.lutron.com/a/documents/3691335_eng.pdf and
https://assets.lutron.com/a/documents/3691301_eng.pdf.

Fan speed controls require a compatible AC paddle fan. DC, smart or
remote-controlled fans need a specific control plan. Verify load and neutral
for the selected control. Source: Lutron 3691169,
https://assets.lutron.com/a/documents/3691169_eng.pdf.

Recorded layout review can add RA3 processors and Type A repeaters for
coverage within manufacturer limits. HomeWorks review chooses the wireless
processor or the wired QSX plus gateway architecture. Wired shades count
against QS link address and output allowances.

EXISTING-LIGHT CONTROL UPGRADES
Dimmed and on/off group counts retain existing fixtures. Include removal of
old wall controls, replacement masters and configured companions, circuit
assessment, device installation and applicable commissioning. Existing local
controls reuse suitable lighting wiring. New control locations add boxes and
relocation runs. Centralization is an explicit keypad-only design choice and
adds panel load runs, modules and shared equipment. Existing Lutron systems
still require compatibility/capacity review before release.
